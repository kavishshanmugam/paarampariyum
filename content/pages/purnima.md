# Purnima (Full Moon)

## In two sentences

Purnima is the fifteenth and final tithi of the bright fortnight, the full moon day that closes the waxing cycle and opens the waning one. Every month's Purnima has its own name and associated observance, but all share a common thread: bathing, charity, fasting, and the Satyanarayan Puja are traditional across traditions.

## The twelve Purnimas

Each month's Purnima carries a distinct name and significance:

| Month (Amanta) | Purnima Name | Key Association |
|---|---|---|
| Chaitra | Chaitra Purnima | Hanuman Jayanti (in most traditions) |
| Vaishakha | Vaishakha Purnima / Buddha Purnima | Birth, enlightenment, and passing of the Buddha; sacred to Hindus as Vishnu's day |
| Jyeshtha | Vat Purnima | Married women observe Savitri vrat, tying threads to a banyan tree |
| Ashadha | Guru Purnima | Veneration of one's guru and the sage Vyasa (Vyas Purnima) |
| Shravana | Shravana Purnima / Raksha Bandhan | Rakhi tied; Avani Avittam (sacred thread renewal, South India) |
| Bhadrapada | Bhadra Purnima | Pitra Paksha begins the day after |
| Ashvina | Sharad Purnima / Kojagari Purnima | Moon said to shower nectar (amrit); kheer left overnight in moonlight |
| Kartika | Kartika Purnima / Dev Deepawali | Lamps lit on the Ganga; Guru Nanak Jayanti |
| Margashirsha | Margashirsha Purnima | Dattatreya Jayanti in some traditions |
| Pausha | Pausha Purnima | Begins the Magha mela bathing season |
| Magha | Magha Purnima | Bathing in sacred rivers; Satyanarayan Puja especially common |
| Phalguna | Phalguna Purnima / Holi Purnima | Holika Dahan |

## The story

**Short version.** The full moon (Chandra) is the vessel of Soma, the nourishing essence that the Vedas describe as the food of the gods. On Purnima, Chandra is at his fullest; the tides are highest and, traditional understanding holds, the body and mind are most responsive to spiritual practice.

**Full version.** The Vedic Soma is connected to the moon in the Rig and Atharva Vedas. The Chandra Upanishad and the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad both describe the moon as the repository of consciousness between births -- departed souls reside in the moon during the intermediate period. This gives Purnima its dual character: it is at once the most auspicious day for living (full vitality, full lunar energy) and a day for remembering the dead (the boundary between worlds is thinner).

The Satyanarayan Puja -- an extended form of Vishnu puja accompanied by the reading of the Satyanarayan Katha from the Skandha Purana -- has become the most common Purnima observance in North Indian and diaspora families. It can be performed at home with a small group and requires modest materials.

Kartika Purnima holds special importance: it falls on the full moon closest to the winter solstice in India, and the Ganga ghats are lit with lakhs of lamps (Dev Deepawali). Sharad Purnima (Ashvina) is when Chandra is said to be closest to earth and radiates amrit; leaving kheer (sweetened rice pudding) exposed to the moonlight overnight is a beloved tradition.

*Sources: Rigveda (Soma hymns), Brihadaranyaka Upanishad, Skanda Purana (Satyanarayan Katha), various regional Puranas*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. In the evening, step outside and look at the full moon; offer water from a vessel toward it while saying "Om Som Somaya Namah" (the moon mantra)
2. Light a lamp at the home shrine
3. If you have time, read one story from the Satyanarayan Katha (widely available in print and audio)
4. Give something in charity -- even a small donation counts on Purnima

### Fuller version

**Satyanarayan Puja (the most common Purnima observance):**
1. Gather a small group of family or friends, or do it alone
2. Prepare the puja thali: panchamrit, flowers, turmeric, kumkum, fruits, sweets (sheera / sooji halwa is traditional for Satyanarayan puja)
3. Set up an image of Vishnu / Satyanarayana
4. Read or listen to all five chapters of the Satyanarayan Katha (the Skanda Purana version) -- this takes about 45-60 minutes
5. After the katha, distribute prasad (sheera/halwa) to everyone present
6. In the evening, go outside at moonrise; offer water to the moon

**For Vrat (fast) observers:**
- Fast from grains during the day; break fast after moonrise and prasad
- Milk, fruit, and vrat-appropriate foods (sabudana, sendha namak, rajgira) are permitted

**Sharad Purnima (Ashvina Purnima) special:**
- Prepare kheer (rice cooked in milk with sugar and cardamom)
- Set it outdoors in the moonlight for a few hours after moonrise
- Bring it inside and serve as prasad -- this is considered especially nourishing

### Items needed

- Small water vessel (copper preferred) for moon offering
- Lamp and incense
- Satyanarayan Katha book (or audio -- widely available in Hindi, Telugu, Kannada)
- Sheera/halwa ingredients for prasad: semolina, ghee, sugar, milk, cardamom
- Flowers and fruit

## Why

**Scriptural.** Purnima is described in the Dharmashastra texts as a parva (sacred junction day) along with Amavasya and the two Ekadashis of each month. Charity, fasting, and worship on parva days are held to yield many times the merit of the same acts on ordinary days.

**Symbolic.** The full moon as vessel of completeness -- the Vedic phrase "Purnasya Purnam adaya Purnam evavashishyate" (taking the complete from the complete, completeness remains) is associated with the full moon. Purnima is the natural time to feel and celebrate completion in any endeavour.

**Physiological lore.** Traditional Ayurvedic texts (Charaka Samhita, Ashtanga Hridayam) note that the full moon influences tides and body fluids. Fasting or eating lightly on Purnima is recommended as a monthly reset. This is a traditional view; individuals should make their own judgement.

## How others do it

- **South India (Tamil, Telugu, Kannada):** Pournami is observed with temple visits; Pradosha-adjacent purnimas and Karthigai Deepam (Kartika Purnima) are especially important in Tamil tradition
- **Vaishnava traditions:** Purnima is Vishnu's day; Satyanarayan Puja, ekadashi observances near it, and the Vishnu Sahasranama recitation are common
- **Buddhist tradition:** Purnima is Uposatha day -- the full and new moon days of moral renewal for lay Buddhists; Theravada and Tibetan communities both observe
- **Bengali tradition:** Lokkhi Puja (Lakshmi Puja) is performed on every Purnima by many households, especially in autumn

## Who is traditionally exempt

The fast associated with Purnima is optional and varies by region and tradition. Those who are pregnant, nursing, seriously ill, very young, or elderly are exempt from any fasting. The charitable act (dan) and the moon offering are accessible to everyone.
