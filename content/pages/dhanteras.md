# Dhanteras

## In two sentences

The first evening of Diwali, when Dhanvantari — the divine physician — is honoured alongside Lakshmi, and homes are lit with lamps to ward off Yama, the god of death. Families traditionally buy gold, silver, or new utensils as a gesture of welcoming prosperity.

## In Melbourne

The engine computes this for Melbourne's coordinates and AEDT timezone. Dhanteras falls two days before the new moon of Kartika, placing it in mid-spring in Melbourne. The custom of buying something auspicious translates naturally — many families visit jewellers or buy a piece of silverware before lighting the evening lamps.

## The story

**Short version.** Dhanvantari emerged from the churning of the cosmic ocean carrying a pot of amrita (nectar of immortality), making this day auspicious for health and wealth. A lamp is also lit to appease Yama, seeking protection from untimely death.

**Full version.** According to the Bhagavata Purana, when the gods and demons churned the cosmic ocean (Samudra Manthan), Dhanvantari arose on the Trayodashi of Ashvina Krishna Paksha, bearing the vessel of amrita and the knowledge of Ayurveda. Because he is the origin of medicine and the sustainer of life, this day is considered deeply auspicious for health and for the beginning of new endeavours. A parallel legend from the Skanda Purana tells of a young king whose early death was prophesied. His wife stayed awake through the night of Trayodashi, heaping gold and silver at the door and singing loudly to confuse Yama's messenger. The oil lamp lit at the entrance is said to be Yamaraj's lamp — a respectful acknowledgement of death and a prayer for the health of the household.

*Sources: Bhagavata Purana (Book 8), Skanda Purana, Vishnu Purana*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. Clean the entrance of the home in the late afternoon
2. At dusk, light a diya at the front door, facing south (towards Yama's direction)
3. Offer a brief prayer to Dhanvantari for the health of the household
4. If buying something is not possible, place a coin or a small piece of silver at the shrine as a token

### Fuller version

1. Begin cleaning the home — Dhanteras traditionally starts the Diwali cleaning
2. In the afternoon, purchase gold, silver, new utensils, or at minimum a small coin — the act of buying metal is considered an invitation to Lakshmi
3. Bathe before the evening puja
4. Set up the puja area with images of Dhanvantari and Lakshmi
5. Light a 13-wick lamp (trayodashi — thirteen flames for the thirteenth tithi) if you follow this custom
6. Light a single diya at the entrance, facing south
7. Offer flowers, akshat (rice), and a sweet to Dhanvantari; recite "Om Dhanvantaraye Namah"
8. Perform Lakshmi puja with incense, flowers, and kumkum
9. In some families, the new purchase is placed before Lakshmi and blessed

### Items needed

- Oil lamp (diya) or 13-wick lamp
- Wicks and oil or ghee
- Flowers (marigold or lotus)
- Akshat (unbroken rice, optionally coloured with turmeric)
- Sweets for offering
- New purchase or coin to place at the shrine
- Incense (optional)

### In Melbourne

Indian grocers in Footscray, Dandenong, and Clayton carry diyas, wicks, and puja supplies. For the traditional purchase of gold or silver, Indian jewellers in the CBD and Dandenong are well stocked in the Diwali season. Even a small silver coin from a jeweller is considered sufficient.

## Why

**Scriptural.** Dhanvantari's emergence on this tithi is recorded in the Bhagavata Purana; the lamp for Yama is mentioned in the Skanda Purana. The purchase of metal is linked to Lakshmi's love of cleanliness, light, and material order.

**Symbolic.** Dhan means wealth; teras is a corruption of Trayodashi, the thirteenth. Health (Dhanvantari) and prosperity (Lakshmi) are honoured together because neither is complete without the other. The south-facing lamp is an act of honest acknowledgement: death exists, and we ask only for sufficient time and health.

**Seasonal.** In India, the harvest is freshly in and the trading year is about to close. Buying metal was also practically sound — converting surplus grain income into durable assets. In Melbourne spring, the spirit of fresh beginning fits the season well.

## How others do it

- **South India:** Dhanteras is observed but the main Deepavali celebration is Naraka Chaturdashi the following morning; emphasis on oil bath rather than lamp purchase
- **Gujarat:** Dhanteras begins an extended five-day celebration; the purchase of utensils is particularly emphasised
- **Maharashtra:** Abhyanga snan (oil bath) is also done on Dhanteras morning in some families
- **Ayurvedic practitioners:** This is formally celebrated as Dhanvantari Jayanti — the birthday of the patron deity of medicine

## Who is traditionally exempt

There is no fast on Dhanteras. Those who are unwell or elderly are not expected to stay up late. Children participate with the lighting of lamps.
