# Ram Navami

## In two sentences

The ninth day of the bright fortnight in Chaitra, observed as the birth of Rama -- seventh avatar of Vishnu, ideal king, son, husband, and friend -- at midday in Ayodhya. The day is marked by reading or listening to the Ramayana, fasting or feasting, and special temple arti at noon.

## In Melbourne

The engine computes this for Melbourne's coordinates and AEDT timezone. Chaitra Shukla Navami falls in late March or April, which in Melbourne is early-to-mid autumn. Many families mark it with a midday puja timed to the local noon, as the Ramayana specifies that Rama was born when the sun was at its zenith.

## The story

**Short version.** King Dasharatha of Ayodhya performed the Putrakameshti yajna (fire ritual for sons) after years without an heir. The gods brought Payasam (sacred pudding) from the fire; Dasharatha's three wives drank it. At noon on the Navami, Kaushalya gave birth to Rama, who bore the marks of Vishnu. The sun, the moon, and the planets were in auspicious positions.

**Full version.** The Valmiki Ramayana opens with this scene (Bala Kanda, Sargas 18-19). Dasharatha, ruler of the solar dynasty, lacked a successor and was advised by the sage Vashishtha and the visiting sage Rishyashringa to perform the Putrakameshti yajna. The fire deity appeared and gave a vessel of divine Payasam. Rama was born to Kaushalya, Bharata to Kaikeyi, and the twins Lakshmana and Shatrughna to Sumitra.

The Adhyatma Ramayana (embedded in the Brahmanda Purana) and the Ramcharitmanas of Tulsidas both elaborate on the celestial joy at Rama's birth -- rishis showered flowers, gandharvas sang, and the three worlds rejoiced. Rama is not merely a king; he is Maryada Purushottam -- the highest exemplar of righteous conduct within the limits of human duty.

The Ramcharitmanas (Tulsidas, 16th c.) is the text most widely read aloud on this day in North India, particularly the Bal Kanda. In South India, recitation of the Valmiki Ramayana is preferred. The Akhanda (unbroken) reading of the entire Ramayana over nine days, starting Pratipada and ending on Navami, is a common congregational practice.

*Sources: Valmiki Ramayana (Bala Kanda), Ramcharitmanas (Tulsidas), Adhyatma Ramayana*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. At midday: light a lamp and offer flowers at a picture or murti of Rama, Sita, Lakshmana, and Hanuman
2. Chant "Sri Ram Jai Ram Jai Jai Ram" for a few minutes -- this is from the Ram Raksha Stotra and is one of the most accessible Rama mantras
3. Read or listen to one sarga (chapter) of the Ramayana -- the birth scene (Bala Kanda, Sarga 18) is the natural choice
4. Share a fruit or sweet as prasad

### Fuller version

1. If fasting: avoid grains; fruit, milk, nuts, and root vegetables are permitted
2. In the morning: bathe, set up the altar with Rama's image, place flowers (lotus, marigold, tulsi)
3. Read the Ramayana -- even a few verses aloud is meaningful; the Sundar Kanda or Bal Kanda birth scene is common
4. At noon precisely: perform the birth celebration (Janmotsav) -- ring a bell or blow a conch if you have one, offer Payasam (sweet rice pudding) as prasad
5. Sing Ram bhajans: "Sri Ram Chandra Kripalu", "Hum Ko Man Ki Shakti Dena", "Raghupati Raghava Raja Ram"
6. In the evening: attend temple if possible; many run special programmes on this day
7. Break the fast in the evening after a final prayer

### Items needed

- Image or murti of Rama (ideally with Sita, Lakshmana, Hanuman)
- Tulsi leaves (sacred to Vishnu)
- Yellow or orange flowers (marigold, lotus, or rose)
- Payasam / kheer ingredients: rice, milk, sugar, cardamom
- Incense and lamp

### In Melbourne

The Sri Siva Vishnu Temple in Carrum Downs, the Swaminarayan Temple in Glen Waverley, and ISKCON Melbourne (Albert Park) all hold Ram Navami programmes with special noon arti and community feasts. Tulsi plants and Indian sweets for prasad are available from Footscray and Dandenong grocers. If attending a temple, check the programme ahead -- some run all-day Ramayana readings.

## Why

**Scriptural.** Rama is the seventh avatar of Vishnu, born to restore dharma when it has declined. His birth on Navami is described in the Valmiki Ramayana as a cosmic event, not merely a royal birth.

**Symbolic.** Rama as Maryada Purushottam embodies the ideal of a person who upholds righteousness even at personal cost -- exile, separation from his wife, war. His story is not a comfort fable; it is a study in how to remain dharmic under pressure.

**The noon timing.** The midday puja is not arbitrary. The Ramayana specifies the astronomical moment of Rama's birth: sun in Aries, moon in Cancer, Jupiter with the moon, Venus in Pisces. The noon reading and celebration are a way of connecting to that precise moment across time.

**Hanuman's role.** Ram Navami is inseparable from Hanuman -- the ideal devotee. The day recalls not just Rama's birth but the bhakti relationship between them. This is why Hanuman Chalisa is often recited on this day too.

## How others do it

- **Ayodhya:** A ten-day mela (fair); the Ram Janmabhoomi temple draws enormous pilgrimage; grand procession (shobha yatra) with tableau of Ramayana scenes
- **Andhra Pradesh / Telangana:** Sri Ramnavami Utsavam -- classical music and dance performances (Carnatic concerts) dedicated to Rama; a nine-day festival with chariot procession on the final day
- **Tamil Nadu:** Temples perform special abishekam; the Ramayana is read in Tamil (Kamba Ramayanam)
- **Maharashtra:** Ram Navami often coincides with or is close to Gudi Padwa; the connection is celebrated
- **ISKCON / Vaishnava tradition worldwide:** Grand celebrations with kirtan, recitation, and feast; Payasam and panchamrit distributed as prasad

## Who is traditionally exempt

Fasting is optional and not prescribed for everyone. Children, pregnant women, the elderly, and anyone in poor health are fully exempt. The core observance is the listening or reading of the Ramayana and the midday puja -- both are accessible to everyone.
