# Karthigai Deepam

## In two sentences

The Tamil festival of lamps, falling on the full moon of Karthigai when the Krittika stars are ascendant. Rows of oil lamps are lit at every window, doorstep, and wall niche -- this, not Diwali, is the true lamp festival of the Tamil tradition.

## In Melbourne

Karthigai Deepam falls in November-December, early summer in Melbourne with long twilight evenings. The challenge is lighting dozens of outdoor lamps in Melbourne's occasional November wind -- sheltered spots or glass lamp holders help. The Sri Siva Vishnu Temple (Carrum Downs) may hold special Deepam events.

## The story

**Short version.** Shiva manifested as an infinite column of fire (Jyotirlinga) to settle a dispute between Brahma and Vishnu about who was supreme. Neither could find its end. Every lamp lit on Karthigai Deepam represents that boundless light.

**Full version.** The Shiva Purana (Vidyeshvara Samhita) and Linga Purana tell the story. Brahma and Vishnu argued over supremacy. A blazing pillar of light appeared between them. Brahma flew upward as a swan; Vishnu dove downward as Varaha. Neither found the end. Shiva revealed himself from within, proving he was beyond both. Brahma lied about reaching the top; Shiva cursed him to have no temples and the Ketaki flower to never be used in Shiva worship.

The **Tiruvannamalai Mahadeepam** is the most spectacular observance: a massive fire is lit on top of Arunachala hill using tons of ghee and camphor, visible for miles. The Arunachaleshvara Temple below -- one of the Pancha Bhuta Sthalas (temple of the fire element) -- holds a ten-day festival culminating in the Deepam. Millions attend.

The Krittika nakshatra connection: the six Krittika stars are the Karthigai Pengal (six mothers) who raised Murugan after his birth from six sparks of Shiva's third eye. Karthigai Deepam also honours Murugan's foster mothers.

In Tamil homes, the tradition is to light rows of agal vilakku (clay lamps) at every window, doorstep, and wall niche. The saying is "Karthigai maadam vilakku" -- the Karthigai month lamp. Unlike Deepavali (which in Tamil Nadu is the morning oil bath on Naraka Chaturdashi), Karthigai Deepam is the Tamil festival of lamps proper.

*Sources: Shiva Purana (Vidyeshvara Samhita, Chapters 6-9), Linga Purana, Skanda Purana (Arunachala Mahatmya), Periya Puranam*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. At sunset, light clay lamps (agal vilakku) at every window and doorway -- as many as you can
2. Light a tall lamp (kuthu vilakku) at the home shrine
3. Offer camphor to Shiva
4. Chant "Om Namah Shivaya"

### Fuller version

1. Clean the house; wash all lamp holders
2. Prepare ghee wicks or sesame oil wicks for clay lamps
3. In the evening (pradosh kalam -- twilight), light the main lamp at the shrine first
4. Light a lamp at every window, doorstep, niche, compound wall, and courtyard
5. The kuthu vilakku (standing brass lamp with multiple tiers) is the central lamp
6. Cook traditional foods: Pori (puffed rice), Appam, Adirasam (deep-fried jaggery-rice sweet), Seedai, Murukku
7. In some families, a bonfire is lit in the courtyard
8. Sing Thevaram (Shaiva devotional hymns by the Nayanar saints) or Thiruppugazh (for Murugan)
9. Visit the temple for special Deepa aradhana (lamp worship)
10. Exchange sweets and lamps with neighbours

### Items needed

- Clay lamps (agal vilakku) -- as many as possible; dozens is traditional
- Ghee or sesame oil (gingelly oil) for lamps
- Cotton wicks
- Camphor
- Kuthu vilakku (standing brass lamp) -- optional but traditional
- Adirasam, seedai, pori, appam ingredients
- Incense, flowers

### In Melbourne

Clay lamps are available cheaply at Indian stores, especially October-December. Sesame oil (gingelly oil / nallennai) is standard stock at Indian and Sri Lankan grocers. For the traditional adirasam, frozen versions are sometimes available at South Indian stores in Dandenong.

## Why

**Scriptural.** The Jyotirlinga manifestation is foundational to Shaiva tradition. The Arunachala Mahatmya (Skanda Purana) states that lighting a lamp on Karthigai is equivalent to worshipping at Arunachala.

**Symbolic.** Each lamp represents the infinite light (Jyoti) that is Shiva -- without beginning, without end. Lighting many lamps from a single flame symbolises the one Brahman appearing as many individual souls.

**Seasonal.** In Tamil Nadu, Karthigai (November-December) is post-monsoon; nights are dark and clear. In Melbourne, late November is early summer with long daylight, so the lamps are most striking at twilight.

## How others do it

- **Tamil Nadu:** Full lamp display at every home; Tiruvannamalai Deepam is the culmination; Murugan temples hold special events
- **Andhra/Telangana:** Kartika Deepotsavam; vanabhojana (garden picnic) on Kartika Purnima
- **Karnataka:** Kartika lamps are lit but less elaborately than in Tamil Nadu
- **Kerala:** Observed at temples but less prominent as a home festival
- **Distinct from Deepavali:** In North India, Diwali (Kartika Amavasya) is the lamp festival. In Tamil Nadu, Deepavali is the oil bath morning and Karthigai Deepam (two weeks later, on Purnima) is the lamp festival.

## Who is traditionally exempt

No fasting is required. Anyone may light lamps. Those who cannot manage many lamps may light just one.
