# Vasant Panchami

## In two sentences

The fifth day of the bright fortnight in Magha, when Saraswati -- goddess of learning, speech, music, and the arts -- is worshipped and the arrival of spring (vasant ritu) is celebrated. The traditional colour of the day is yellow, worn in clothing and offered in food, echoing the mustard fields blooming across North India.

It is an excellent day to dedicate to learning something new.

## The story

**Short version.** Brahma created the world but found it silent and joyless. He sprinkled water from his kamandalu (water pot); from it arose Saraswati, holding a veena (lute), books, and a rosary, riding a white swan. She played the veena and gave sound to the world -- wind, water, birds, human speech. Vasant Panchami is her birthday.

**Full version.** The story of Saraswati's emergence appears in the Brahma Vaivarta Purana and is elaborated in the Devi Bhagavata Purana. In another version, Saraswati emerged from the tongue of Brahma himself, as the power of speech (Vak) is inseparable from the creator. She is also Vani (speech), Sharada (one who bestows the essence of things), Bharati (speech of the learned), and Vagdevi (goddess of words).

The Rigveda contains the oldest reference to Saraswati as a mighty river and a deity of wisdom. By the Puranic period she had become fully associated with learning, music, and the arts. The white swan (hamsa) that is her vahana (mount) is said to be able to separate milk from water -- symbolising the discrimination (viveka) that true learning develops.

Vasant Panchami is also considered an auspicious date for Vidyarambha (Vidhyarambham) -- the ceremony marking a child's first writing lesson. The child traces the letters "Hari Sri Ganapataye Namah" on a plate of rice or on a slate, often guided by the teacher's hand. This ceremony is especially prominent in Kerala (Vijayadasami is also used) and Bengal.

*Sources: Brahma Vaivarta Purana, Devi Bhagavata Purana, Rigveda (Mandala 2, 6)*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. Wear something yellow -- even a yellow scarf or hair accessory
2. Place the books, instruments, or tools of your work or study before Saraswati's image and offer a flower
3. Chant "Om Aim Saraswatyai Namah" (Saraswati's beej mantra)
4. Eat something yellow -- saffron rice, yellow dal, or sweet boondi
5. If you have children, let them trace their name in a tray of rice as a simple Vidyarambha

### Fuller version

1. Bathe and wear yellow clothes
2. Set up an altar with an image or murti of Saraswati -- ideally white, holding a veena, books, and rosary
3. Place your books, instruments, pens, laptops, or any tools of learning and craft before her (they are not to be used on this day -- they rest in her presence)
4. Offer white flowers (jasmine, white lotus) and yellow flowers (marigold); offer yellow sweets (besan laddoo, boondi)
5. Recite the Saraswati Vandana: "Ya Kundendu Tusharahara Dhavala..."
6. Recite the Saraswati Ashtottara (108 names) if you have it, or simply meditate on the quality of clear, truthful speech
7. If a child is beginning school or a new course of learning, perform Vidyarambha: write the opening invocation in rice or on a slate
8. In the evening, return the books and instruments to use with a short prayer of thanks
9. Prepare or eat sweet yellow rice (kesari bhat, zarda) as prasad

### Items needed

- Image or murti of Saraswati (Devi Mahatmyam or similar book with her image is fine)
- White and yellow flowers
- Yellow sweets or sweet saffron rice
- Incense and lamp
- The books, instruments, or creative tools you want blessed
- A tray of rice (for Vidyarambha, if doing it)

### In Melbourne

Yellow marigolds and jasmine are available year-round at Footscray and Dandenong markets. Indian grocers carry besan laddoo and boondi. The BAPS Swaminarayan Temple (Glen Waverley) and the Durga temple in Rockbank observe Vasant Panchami with cultural programmes -- music performances and children's learning ceremonies are common.

## Why

**Scriptural.** Saraswati is the power of Vak (speech) without which knowledge cannot be transmitted. The Rigveda describes her as "the inspirer of all pleasant songs, the awakener of happy thoughts." The Yajurveda Taittiriya Upanishad opens with a prayer to Vak.

**Symbolic.** Yellow is the colour of ripe grain, of gold, of the sun at midday -- abundance and clarity. Offering your tools of work to Saraswati is a reminder that skill is a gift, not a possession; it returns you to beginner's mind.

**The swan.** The hamsa's mythic ability to sip only milk from a mixture of milk and water stands for the student's task: to extract the essential from the mass of available information, and to seek truth over noise.

**Seasonal.** In India, Vasant Panchami is the formal announcement of spring. Schools in Bengal and North India hold cultural events; kite flying is common in Gujarat and Rajasthan. The festivals of spring (Holi, Chaitra Navratri) are still weeks away, but the season has officially turned.

## How others do it

- **Bengal:** Saraswati Puja is a major school festival; students dress formally and gather at elaborately decorated community pandals; the goddess is installed and worshipped over two days; Anjali (offering of flowers) is performed by students collectively
- **Punjab / Haryana:** Primarily a spring and kite festival -- yellow clothes, mustard fields in full bloom; the religious significance is lighter than in Bengal
- **South India (Kerala, Tamil Nadu):** Vijayadasami is more commonly used for Vidyarambha; Vasant Panchami is observed but less prominently
- **Bihar / UP:** Books placed before the goddess; students visit the temple; sweet yellow rice prepared at home
- **Rajasthan:** Kite flying competitions; many royal-era celebrations included poetry and music gatherings on this day

## Who is traditionally exempt

There are no fasting requirements associated with Vasant Panchami for most communities -- it is a celebratory day, not an austerity day. The only traditional restriction is that books, instruments, and tools of work are not to be used during the puja period (typically the morning); they rest in Saraswati's presence. Children are the focus of the day's most meaningful rituals.
