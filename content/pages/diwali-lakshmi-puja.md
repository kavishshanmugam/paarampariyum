# Deepavali Lakshmi Puja

## In two sentences

The new moon night of Kartika, when Lakshmi is welcomed with rows of lamps. In South India, the main Deepavali is the previous morning (Naraka Chaturdashi with the oil bath); this Lakshmi Puja evening is the North Indian centrepiece but is observed across traditions.

## The story

**Short version.** In South India, the main Deepavali is the morning before (Naraka Chaturdashi) with the oil bath. This Amavasya evening is when Lakshmi, goddess of wealth and wellbeing, is welcomed with lamps into homes that are clean, lit, and open. In North India, the lamps also recall Rama's return to Ayodhya.

**Full version.** The Ramayana tells that when Rama, Sita and Lakshmana returned to Ayodhya after defeating Ravana in Lanka, the citizens lit rows of earthen lamps to guide them home. The tradition of lighting diyas on Amavasya is also linked to Lakshmi's emergence during the churning of the cosmic ocean (Samudra Manthan), described in the Vishnu Purana and Bhagavata Purana. In Bengal, the night is dedicated to Kali, the fierce form of the Goddess who destroys darkness. In much of South India, the main Deepavali celebration is the previous morning (Naraka Chaturdashi), marking Krishna's victory over the demon Naraka.

*Sources: Ramayana (Yuddha Kanda), Vishnu Purana, Bhagavata Purana, Padma Purana*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. Clean the entrance and draw a small rangoli or kolam
2. At dusk, light at least one oil lamp at the door and one at the home shrine
3. Offer flowers and a sweet to Lakshmi and Ganesha
4. Say a short prayer -- even "Om Shri Mahalakshmyai Namah" is enough
5. Share the sweet as prasad with the household

### Fuller version

1. Clean the home thoroughly in the days before (many families start after Dhanteras)
2. Bathe and wear new or clean clothes before the puja
3. Set up the puja area with images or murtis of Lakshmi and Ganesha
4. Light a five-wick lamp (panch-mukhi diya) if you have one
5. Offer turmeric, kumkum, flowers, fruit, and sweets
6. Recite the Lakshmi Ashtottara Shatanamavali (108 names) or the Shri Suktam
7. In many traditions, account books are placed before Lakshmi (the new financial year)
8. Light lamps at every door and window
9. Distribute sweets to neighbours
10. In some families, a small amount of gambling is traditional on this night

### Items needed

- Oil lamps (diyas) -- clay or brass
- Wicks and oil (sesame or mustard, or ghee)
- Flowers (marigold, lotus, or rose)
- Sweets (home-made or bought)
- Rangoli materials (coloured powder, rice flour, or flower petals)
- Incense (optional)
- Image or murti of Lakshmi and Ganesha

## Why

**Scriptural.** Rama's return to Ayodhya; Lakshmi's blessing of the household that is clean, open, and welcoming.

**Symbolic.** Light over darkness, knowledge over ignorance, hope over despair. The new moon is the darkest night, and the lamps transform it.

**Seasonal.** In India, Diwali marks the end of the monsoon and the beginning of the new business year. Harvest is complete; the year is settled.

**Social-historical.** The festival has been a moment of community celebration, renewal of relationships, and charity for centuries. The custom of settling debts and starting fresh accounts is ancient.

## How others do it

- **Bengal:** Kali Puja on the same night, with worship of the Goddess in her fierce form
- **South India:** The main celebration is the previous morning (Naraka Chaturdashi), with an oil bath before dawn
- **Gujarat:** The new year (Bestu Varas) falls the day after Diwali; Chopda Pujan blesses the account books
- **Maharashtra:** Fort-shaped lamps (killa) and Laxmi Puja
- **Sikhs:** Bandi Chhor Divas, marking Guru Hargobind's release from imprisonment
- **Jains:** Mahavira's liberation (moksha) is commemorated on this night

## Who is traditionally exempt

Fasting on Diwali is not standard practice for most communities. Those who are unwell, elderly, or pregnant are always exempt from any associated fasts. Children participate fully.
