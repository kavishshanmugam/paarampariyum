# Ugadi / Puthandu / Vishu

## In two sentences

The South Indian New Year -- three distinct festivals sharing the theme of new beginnings. Tamil Puthandu and Malayalam Vishu fall when the sun enters sidereal Aries (mid-April); Telugu Ugadi and Kannada Yugadi fall on Chaitra Shukla Pratipada (the lunar new year, March-April).

## In Melbourne

The solar new year (Puthandu/Vishu) falls around April 14, mid-autumn in Melbourne. The Sri Lankan Tamil, Malayalee, Telugu, and Kannada communities in Melbourne all organise separate new year celebrations. April is pleasant in Melbourne and community events are often held in halls and parks.

## The story

**Short version.** The new year marks the beginning of a new cycle. Tamil and Malayalam traditions are solar (the sun enters the first sign of the zodiac); Telugu/Kannada traditions are lunar (the first day of the first month). All share the idea: the coming year contains all six tastes of life -- accept them with equanimity.

**Full version.** The Brahma Purana and Surya Siddhanta identify this period as the beginning of creation. The astronomer Varahamihira (6th century CE) codified it as the astronomical new year.

**Puthandu (Tamil):** The key custom is the Kanni -- a tray of auspicious items arranged the night before (mirror, gold jewelry, flowers, fruits, coins, sacred text) so it is the first thing seen on New Year morning. Mango Pachadi (combining sweet, sour, bitter, pungent) is prepared and shared, representing the year's mixed experiences.

**Vishu (Malayalam):** The Vishukkani -- an elaborate arrangement in a brass uruli: golden cucumber, rice, coconut, flowers (konna/golden shower, the Vishu flower), gold coins, mirror, lamp. The senior woman of the house prepares it and leads each family member to see it first thing. Elders give Vishukaineetam (money) to younger members.

**Ugadi (Telugu/Kannada):** The Ugadi Pachadi/Bevu-Bella -- a mixture of six tastes explicitly mapped: neem (sadness), jaggery (happiness), green chili (anger), salt (fear), tamarind (disgust), raw mango (surprise). Each spoonful is the year in miniature. Mango leaf toranas (garlands) decorate the doorway.

*Sources: Brahma Purana, Surya Siddhanta, Varahamihira's Pancha-Siddhantika*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

**Tamil Puthandu:**
1. Arrange a tray (Kanni) with a mirror, coins, flowers, fruits, and a sacred text the night before
2. On New Year morning, see the Kanni first thing
3. Prepare Mango Pachadi and share it

**Malayalam Vishu:**
1. Arrange the Vishukkani in a brass plate with golden cucumber, rice, coconut, flowers, coins, mirror, lamp
2. Open eyes to the Vishukkani first thing
3. Give/receive Vishukaineetam (money)

**Telugu/Kannada Ugadi:**
1. Mix neem flowers, jaggery, tamarind, green chili, salt, and raw mango -- eat a spoonful
2. Hang mango leaf torana at the entrance
3. Listen to the Panchanga Shravanam (year's almanac reading)

### Fuller version

**Puthandu:**
1. Clean the house the day before; draw a large kolam at the entrance
2. Arrange the Kanni tray the night before in the puja room
3. Wake early; see the Kanni first
4. Bathe, wear new clothes (traditional veshti/sari)
5. Visit the temple; read the Panchangam for the new year
6. Prepare the feast: Mango Pachadi, Payasam, Vadai, rice specialties
7. Visit elders; receive blessings; exchange gifts

**Vishu:**
1. The senior woman prepares the Vishukkani the night before, covers it with cloth
2. She wakes first, lights the lamp, opens her eyes to the Kani
3. She leads each family member (eyes closed) to see it
4. Elders give Vishukaineetam
5. Vishu Sadya (feast on banana leaf, similar to Onam Sadya but simpler)
6. Temple visit; fireworks are traditional for Vishu
7. The konna flower (golden shower) is the defining flower

**Ugadi:**
1. Oil bath at dawn; new clothes
2. Mango leaf torana at the entrance; rangoli/muggu
3. Ugadi Pachadi prepared and shared by all
4. Temple visit; Panchanga Shravanam
5. Prepare Pulihora (tamarind rice), Bobbatlu/Obbattu (sweet flatbread), Payasam
6. Visit elders

### Items needed

- Mirror, gold jewelry or coins, fresh flowers, fruits, sacred text (for Kanni/Vishukkani)
- Raw green mango (available at Indian/Sri Lankan grocers)
- Neem flowers or leaves (Ugadi; dried neem available at health stores)
- Jaggery, tamarind, green chili, salt
- Mango leaves for torana
- Banana leaves (for Vishu Sadya)
- Konna flowers (golden shower -- substitute bright yellow flowers in Melbourne)
- New clothes

### In Melbourne

Raw green mangoes are available at Indian/Sri Lankan grocers in season (November-March in Melbourne -- right timing for Pongal, slightly out for the April new year). Neem is the hardest item -- dried neem from health stores works. Mango leaves are occasionally found at flower markets. Tamil, Malayalam, Telugu, and Kannada associations all organise community new year events.

## Why

**Scriptural.** The Brahma Purana identifies Chaitra Shukla Pratipada as the day Brahma began creation. The solar transit into Aries marks the astronomical new year.

**Symbolic.** The six-taste Pachadi is a profound teaching: the coming year will contain happiness, sadness, anger, fear, disgust, and wonder. The tradition says: taste them all at once, accept them with equanimity. The Kanni/Vishukkani -- the first sight of the new year should be auspicious, beautiful, and abundant.

**Seasonal.** In India, mid-April marks the transition to summer and the beginning of the agricultural year. In Melbourne, April is autumn -- the season mapping is reversed, but the new-beginning energy translates.

## Prayers and Mantras

### Vinayagar Vandana
**Tamil:** ஓம் கம் கணபதயே நமஹ
**Transliteration:** Om Gam Ganapataye Namaha
**Meaning:** "Om, salutations to Ganapati."
Every new beginning in Hindu tradition starts with Vinayagar. This mantra is chanted at the start of the new year before any other prayer, to remove obstacles for the year ahead.

### Panchanga Shravanam Prayer
**Tamil:** ஸர்வாரிஷ்ட ப்ரசமனம் ஸர்வ சுப பலப்ரதம் | சர்வ பாப ப்ரசமனம் பஞ்சாங்க ஶ்ரவணம் ஶுபம் ||
**Transliteration:** Sarvarishta Prashamanam Sarva Shubha Phala Pradam | Sarva Papa Prashamanam Panchanga Shravanam Shubham
**Meaning:** "Listening to the Panchanga quells all calamities, grants all auspicious results, and removes all sins."
Recited before the ritual reading of the new year's almanac (Panchanga Shravanam), a key Ugadi tradition where a priest reads the forecast for the coming year.

### Auspicious Beginning Mantra
**Tamil:** ஓம் சர்வமங்கல மாங்கல்யே ஶிவே சர்வார்த்த சாதிகே | ஶரண்யே த்ர்யம்பகே கௌரி நாராயணி நமோஸ்துதே ||
**Transliteration:** Om Sarva Mangala Mangalye Shive Sarvartha Sadhike | Sharanye Tryambake Gauri Narayani Namostute
**Meaning:** "O auspicious of all that is auspicious, O consort of Shiva, who fulfils all purposes — O refuge, three-eyed Gauri, Narayani, I bow to you."
From the Devi Mahatmyam. A universal prayer for auspicious beginnings, widely chanted on new year mornings across South Indian traditions.

## How others do it

- **Tamil Nadu:** Puthandu with Kanni tray and Mango Pachadi
- **Kerala:** Vishu with Vishukkani and Vishukaineetam
- **Andhra/Telangana:** Ugadi with Pachadi and Panchanga Shravanam
- **Karnataka:** Yugadi with Bevu-Bella
- **Maharashtra:** Gudi Padwa (same lunar date as Ugadi, different customs)
- **Punjab:** Vaisakhi (same solar date as Puthandu, different customs)

## Who is traditionally exempt

No fasting or restriction in any of these new year festivals. They are pure celebration days.
