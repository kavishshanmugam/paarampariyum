# Vijayadashami

## In two sentences

Vijayadashami is the tenth and culminating day of Navaratri, celebrated across South India as the most auspicious day to begin learning. For Tamil families, this day centres on Vidyarambham (initiation of children into education), Aayudha Puja (worship of tools and vehicles), and the ceremonial taking down of the Golu display.

## In Melbourne

Vijayadashami falls in September-October (Melbourne spring). The Murugan Temple in Cheltenham and the Shiva Temple in Carrum Downs conduct formal Vidyarambham ceremonies -- families queue with young children to have a priest guide the child's first letters. Many Melbourne Tamil families also perform Vidyarambham at home. Some Tamil professionals perform a small Aayudha Puja at their workplace, blessing laptops and tools of trade.

## The story

**Short version.** On the tenth day of Navaratri, Saraswati -- who received worship on Days 7-9 -- releases books, instruments, and tools back to their owners. Children are initiated into learning (Vidyarambham), tools are blessed (Aayudha Puja), and the Golu display is taken down. In South India, the day is about the triumph of knowledge and the auspiciousness of new beginnings.

**Full version.** The name "Vijayadashami" means "victorious tenth day" (vijaya = victory, dashami = tenth). While North India associates this day with Rama's victory over Ravana (Dussehra), the South Indian tradition centres on Saraswati and the power of learning.

The **Vidyarambham** (Tamil: வித்யாரம்பம், "beginning of knowledge") or **Aksharabhyasam** (அக்ஷராப்யாசம், "practice of letters") is the heart of the day for Tamil families. Children between ages 2 and 5 are formally initiated into learning. An elder -- typically a grandparent, parent, or priest -- holds the child's hand and traces "Om" (ஓம்) or "Hari Sri" (ஹரி ஸ்ரீ) on a plate of rice or in sand. In some traditions, the elder writes on the child's tongue with a gold ring dipped in honey. This moment marks the child's entry into the world of knowledge under Saraswati's blessing.

**Saraswati Puja** reaches its conclusion on Vijayadashami. Books, musical instruments, and tools of trade that were placed near the Golu on Ashtami (Day 8) for Saraswati's blessing are now retrieved. Work and study resume -- the first act of reading or writing after the pause is considered especially auspicious.

**Aayudha Puja** (ஆயுத பூஜை, "worship of implements") is a major observance in Tamil Nadu. All instruments of livelihood are worshipped: vehicles, machinery, computers, kitchen tools, agricultural implements. Vehicles are washed, decorated with turmeric, kumkum, and garlands. Factories and workshops in Tamil Nadu close for Aayudha Puja. The principle is gratitude -- the tools that sustain one's livelihood deserve reverence.

The **Golu** is taken down on Vijayadashami evening. Dolls are carefully wrapped and stored until the next Navaratri. Some families offer a final prayer and distribute the remaining sundal and tambulam before dismantling the steps.

Vijayadashami is also considered the single most auspicious day in the year to begin any new endeavour -- a new course of study, a new business, learning a new skill, or starting a creative project.

*Sources: Devi Mahatmya (Markandeya Purana), Devi Bhagavata Purana, Dharmasindhu, Nirnaya Sindhu*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. Place a plate of raw rice before a lamp and an image of Saraswati
2. Have a child (or yourself) write "Om" or "Hari Sri" on the rice with a finger
3. Retrieve any books or tools set aside during Saraswati Puja
4. Read the first line of a book or write the first word -- resume learning
5. Apply turmeric and kumkum to your primary work tool or vehicle

### Fuller version

**Morning -- Vidyarambham:**
1. Bathe the child and dress them in new or clean clothes
2. Set up a writing surface: a plate of raw rice, or a tray of sand
3. Seat the child facing east, on an elder's lap
4. The elder holds the child's hand and writes "Om" (ஓம்) or "Hari Sri" (ஹரி ஸ்ரீ) on the rice
5. Some families also have the child write on a slate with chalk
6. Offer the child a taste of honey (mixed with ghee, touched with a gold ring to the tongue)
7. Give the child a pencil and notebook as a gift -- their first "tools of learning"
8. Seek blessings from Saraswati for the child's education

**Morning -- Saraswati Puja conclusion:**
1. Retrieve books, instruments, and tools placed for puja on Ashtami/Mahanavami
2. Open a book and read the first line -- this is the formal resumption of study
3. Play the first notes on any musical instrument that was placed for puja

**Morning/afternoon -- Aayudha Puja:**
1. Clean vehicles, tools, and implements of your trade
2. Apply turmeric paste and kumkum to each item
3. Decorate with garlands or mango leaves
4. Place a coconut, banana, and incense before the items
5. Break a coconut and offer a prayer of gratitude
6. In Melbourne, this can be done for your car, laptop, kitchen knives, or any tools you rely on

**Evening -- Golu dismantling:**
1. Light the lamp one final time before the Golu
2. Offer a prayer of thanks to the Goddess
3. Carefully wrap and store each doll
4. Dismantle the steps
5. Store the marapachi bommai (wooden couple) with care -- they return next year

### Items needed

- Plate of raw rice or tray of sand (for Vidyarambham)
- Gold ring and honey (optional, for tongue-writing)
- New notebook and pencil (gift for the child)
- Turmeric paste and kumkum (for Aayudha Puja)
- Flower garlands, mango leaves
- Coconut, bananas, incense
- Lamp and camphor

### In Melbourne

Formal Vidyarambham ceremonies are held at the Murugan Temple (Cheltenham) and the Shiva Vishnu Temple (Carrum Downs) -- arrive early as queues can be long. Many Tamil families perform the ceremony at home with grandparents guiding the child's hand. For Aayudha Puja, turmeric, kumkum, and garlands are available at Indian stores in Dandenong. Some Tamil professionals in Melbourne perform a brief Aayudha Puja at their desks -- a small application of kumkum on the laptop is all it takes.

## Why

**Scriptural.** The Dharmasindhu and Nirnaya Sindhu prescribe Vijayadashami as the foremost muhurta for beginning education. The Devi Bhagavata Purana describes Saraswati as the bestower of all learning and arts.

**Symbolic.** The day marks victory (vijaya) -- not over an external enemy, but over ignorance. The child's first letter on rice is the first step from darkness to light. Aayudha Puja expresses gratitude to the instruments that sustain livelihood, recognising the divine in everyday tools.

**Practical.** Historically, the post-monsoon period was when gurukulas resumed after the Chaturmas break. Vijayadashami marked the formal restart of education. In Tamil Nadu, school admissions traditionally align with this day.

## How others do it

- **Kerala:** Vidyarambham is the central Vijayadashami observance; massive gatherings at Thunchan Parambu (Tirur) where thousands of children are initiated. Books are placed for puja on Day 8 (Pooja Veypu) and retrieved on Day 10 (Puja Eduppu).
- **Karnataka:** Shami Puja -- families exchange shami (banni) leaves as symbolic "gold," recalling the Pandavas retrieving their weapons from a shami tree. Mysore Dasara culminates with the Jamboo Savari procession.
- **Andhra Pradesh / Telangana:** Bommala Koluvu is taken down; Vidyarambham observed; Dussehra also marks the end of the Bathukamma flower festival in Telangana.
- **North India:** Dussehra -- burning of Ravana effigies (Ramlila conclusion); Durga visarjan in Bengal. The emphasis is on Rama's victory, not on Saraswati or learning.

## Who is traditionally exempt

Vijayadashami does not involve fasting. There are no exemptions needed -- it is a day of celebration, not austerity. All ages participate: the very young through Vidyarambham, adults through Aayudha Puja, and elders by guiding the next generation's first steps into learning.
