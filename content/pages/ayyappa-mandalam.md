# Ayyappa Mandala Kalam

## In two sentences

A 41-day period of austerity and devotion to Lord Ayyappa, culminating in the Mandala Puja at Sabarimala and continuing through Makaravilakku in January. Devotees wear black or blue, observe strict vegetarian diet and celibacy, and address each other as "Swami" -- recognising the divine in every pilgrim.

## In Melbourne

The Mandala Kalam runs from mid-November to late December, spanning Melbourne's late spring and early summer. The Ayyappa devotee community in Melbourne organises group worship during this period. Some groups arrange pilgrimages to Sabarimala in December-January.

## The story

**Short version.** Lord Ayyappa is the son of Shiva and Vishnu (in Vishnu's female form, Mohini). Abandoned as a baby, he was found and raised by the king of Pandalam. After extraordinary feats -- including defeating the demoness Mahishi -- he retired to the hilltop of Sabarimala as a celibate yogi. The 41-day observance mirrors his period of penance.

**Full version.** The Bhagavata Purana (Skandha 8) describes Vishnu taking the form of Mohini to distribute amrita after the churning of the ocean. The Shiva Purana and regional texts describe Shiva's attraction to Mohini and the birth of a son -- Dharma Shasta (Ayyappa). The child was abandoned on the banks of the Pampa river with a bell around his neck. The childless king of Pandalam found him and raised him as Manikandan.

When the queen bore a biological son and schemed against Manikandan, she pretended illness and demanded tigress's milk as a cure. Manikandan went to the forest, subdued the demoness Mahishi (who could only be killed by the son of Shiva and Vishnu), and returned riding a tigress, proving his divine nature. He then retreated to the peak of Sabarimala for eternal meditation.

The combination of Shiva and Vishnu in one deity (Hariharaputra) makes Ayyappa theologically unique -- he transcends the Shaiva-Vaishnava divide. The famous chant is "Swamiye Sharanam Ayyappa" (Lord Ayyappa, I take refuge in you).

The 18 steps at Sabarimala represent: the 5 indriyas (senses), 8 ragams (emotions), 3 gunas (qualities), vidya (knowledge), and avidya (ignorance). A pilgrim ascends past all of these to reach the divine. "Tat tvam asi" (That thou art) is inscribed at Sabarimala.

*Sources: Bhagavata Purana (Skandha 8 -- Mohini episode), Brahmanda Purana, Skanda Purana, Bhoothanaatha Upakhyanam (Malayalam)*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. Light a lamp and offer a prayer to Ayyappa
2. Chant "Swamiye Sharanam Ayyappa" 108 times
3. Observe a vegetarian diet for the day
4. If possible, watch a livestream of Sabarimala events

### Fuller version (41-day observance)

1. **Mala Dharanam (Day 1):** Receive the tulasi mala (necklace) from a guru swami at a temple
2. **41 days of austerity:** Wear black or blue clothes; strict vegetarian diet with no onion, garlic, or alcohol; celibacy; sleep on the floor; wake early; bathe twice daily; visit the temple daily for evening bhajan; address all fellow devotees as "Swami"
3. **Irumudi (sacred bundle):** Prepare the two-compartment cloth bundle: front holds coconut filled with ghee (for offering at the sanctum); back holds rice, jaggery, and puja items
4. **The pilgrimage:** Travel to Sabarimala; trek up the 18 sacred steps; darshan of Lord Ayyappa; break the ghee coconut
5. **In the diaspora:** The 41-day austerity is observed at home with daily temple visits; the pilgrimage may be deferred to a future year

### Items needed

- Tulasi mala or rudraksha mala (necklace)
- Black or blue clothing
- Irumudi cloth (two-compartment bundle)
- Coconut, ghee
- Camphor, incense
- Ayyappa bhajan texts
- Vegetarian sattvic food ingredients

### In Melbourne

Tulasi malas and black clothing are available at Indian stores. The Sri Siva Vishnu Temple (Carrum Downs) or dedicated Ayyappa groups hold weekly bhajans during the Mandala season. Melbourne Ayyappa groups can be found through South Indian community networks.

## Why

**Scriptural.** Ayyappa as Hariharaputra (son of both Hari/Vishnu and Hara/Shiva) transcends sectarian divisions. The 41-day discipline is one of the most demanding lay observances in Hinduism.

**Symbolic.** The black clothes = ego-dissolution and equality (all pilgrims look the same). The 18 steps = ascending past the senses, emotions, and qualities to reach the divine. "Tat tvam asi" at Sabarimala = the ultimate Vedantic teaching.

**Seasonal.** The pilgrimage season spans November-January, which is summer in Melbourne. The 41-day discipline during the busy Christmas-New Year period requires real commitment in the diaspora.

## How others do it

- **Kerala:** The epicentre; Sabarimala is in the Western Ghats; lakhs of pilgrims annually
- **Tamil Nadu:** Large following; many undertake the Sabarimala pilgrimage; many Ayyappa temples
- **Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh:** Growing following
- **Gender note:** Historically, women of menstruating age were barred from Sabarimala. The Indian Supreme Court struck down this restriction in 2018. This remains socially contested.

## Who is traditionally exempt

The 41-day austerity is entirely voluntary. Women, children, the elderly, and anyone unable to maintain the discipline may worship Ayyappa at home without the full observance. The pilgrimage is physically demanding and is not expected of those with health limitations.
