# Thaipusam

## In two sentences

On this day, Parvati gave her son Murugan the divine Vel (lance) to defeat the demon Soorapadman. Devotees honour Murugan with processions, offerings, and kavadi -- acts of devotion ranging from carrying milk pots to elaborate physical vows.

## In Melbourne

Thaipusam falls in January-February, mid-summer in Melbourne, making outdoor processions comfortable. The Sri Lankan Tamil community in Melbourne is large and actively celebrates Thaipusam. Check Tamil community groups for events at temples and community halls.

## The story

**Short version.** The Devas were conquered by the demon brothers Soorapadman, Simhamukhan, and Tarakasura. Shiva's son Murugan -- born from six sparks of Shiva's third eye, nursed by the six Krittika stars, merged into one six-faced child by Parvati -- received the Vel (Shakti lance, embodying Parvati's own power) on this day. With it, he defeated Soorapadman and liberated the Devas.

**Full version.** The Skanda Purana and the Tamil Kanda Puranam (Kachiyappa Sivachariyar, 14th century) give the full account. The demon brothers had gained invincibility through severe penance. Only a son of Shiva could defeat them. Six sparks from Shiva's third eye fell into the Saravana lake and became six babies, nursed by the six Krittika stars (Karthigai Pengal). Parvati embraced all six, who merged into one child with six faces -- Shanmukha. On the day of Pushya nakshatra in the month of Thai, Parvati bestowed the Vel upon Murugan.

Soorapadman was split in two by the Vel; the halves became a peacock (Murugan's vahana) and a rooster (his flag emblem).

The **kavadi** tradition comes from the Skanda Purana: the sage Agastya asked his disciple Idumban to carry two hills on a shoulder pole (kavadi). When he set them down at Palani, the boy sitting atop one hill was Murugan. Idumban challenged him and was killed, then revived by Murugan's grace. Devotees carry kavadi in imitation of Idumban's devotion.

Murugan is the Tamil deity par excellence -- "Tamil Kadavul" (God of Tamil). His worship predates the Puranic period, with references in Sangam literature. The Tirumurugaatruppadai (by Nakkeerar, Sangam era) is the oldest extant poem addressed to Murugan.

*Sources: Skanda Purana, Kanda Puranam (Kachiyappa Sivachariyar), Thiruppugazh (Arunagirinathar, 15th century), Tirumurugaatruppadai (Sangam era)*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. Wake early, bathe, and wear clean clothes (saffron or white)
2. Visit a Murugan temple or set up a picture of Murugan with his Vel
3. Offer fresh milk, fruits, and flowers
4. Chant "Om Saravana Bhava" (Murugan's Shadakshari mantra) or recite Thiruppugazh verses
5. Break a coconut at the shrine

### Fuller version

**For most families (non-kavadi):**
1. Visit the Murugan temple
2. Perform special abhishekam with milk
3. Offer panchamirtam (banana, jaggery, honey, ghee, dates)
4. Chant Thiruppugazh or Kanda Sashti Kavasam
5. Eat a vegetarian meal
6. Some families fast (liquids/fruit only) on this day

**For those undertaking kavadi (with experienced community guidance):**
1. **Preparation (10-48 days before):** Strict vegetarian diet; no alcohol; celibacy; daily temple visits; sleeping on the floor
2. **Eve of Thaipusam:** Shave the head (men); full bath with turmeric water
3. **Thaipusam morning:** Carry the kavadi in procession to the temple:
   - Pal kavadi: pot of milk carried on the head
   - Poo kavadi: decorated flower arch on shoulders
   - Vel kavadi: with body piercings (only with experienced guidance and community support)
4. **At the temple:** Abhishekam, break coconuts, offer the milk
5. Community meal; gratitude prayers

### Items needed

- Fresh milk (for abhishekam)
- Coconuts
- Flowers (lotus, jasmine, or any bright flowers)
- Fruits (bananas, oranges, grapes)
- Camphor, incense
- Panchamirtam ingredients: banana, jaggery, honey, ghee, dates, cardamom
- Vel (small brass or silver lance icon -- available at Indian stores)

### In Melbourne

The Sri Lankan Tamil community in Melbourne is the primary organising force for Thaipusam. Malaysian Tamils also observe it. The Sri Siva Vishnu Temple (Carrum Downs) or dedicated Murugan shrines host Thaipusam events. Audio recordings of Thiruppugazh and Kanda Sashti Kavasam are freely available online.

## Why

**Scriptural.** The Vel is Shakti herself in weapon form. The Thiruppugazh (Arunagirinathar, 15th century) is a masterwork of Tamil devotional literature, entirely addressed to Murugan. The Tirumurugaatruppadai (Sangam era) is the oldest poem to Murugan.

**Symbolic.** Murugan represents youth, courage, and the destruction of inner demons. Soorapadman = ego and ignorance. The Vel pierces illusion. The kavadi is a willing offering of the body -- the surrender of physical comfort for spiritual clarity.

**Seasonal.** Thai month (January-February) is mid-summer in Melbourne, making outdoor processions comfortable.

## How others do it

- **Tamil Nadu:** Observed at the six Arupadai Veedu temples (Palani, Tiruchendur, Tirupparan Kundram, Swamimalai, Tiruttani, Pazhamudir Cholai); kavadi is common but not universal
- **Malaysia/Singapore:** Massive celebration at Batu Caves -- the world's largest Thaipusam; silver chariots, millions of devotees
- **Sri Lanka:** Major celebration at Nallur Kandaswamy Temple (Jaffna) and Kataragama
- **Not observed in North India** -- Murugan worship is primarily South Indian and Southeast Asian

## Who is traditionally exempt

Kavadi vows are voluntary and individually chosen. Those who do not take a vow simply visit the temple and pray. Children, the elderly, pregnant/nursing women, and the ill should not carry kavadi. The preparation restrictions apply only to those who have taken a specific vow.
