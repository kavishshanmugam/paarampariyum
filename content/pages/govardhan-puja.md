# Govardhan Puja (Annakut)

## In two sentences

The day after Diwali (Kartika Shukla Pratipada), when Krishna's lifting of Mount Govardhan to shelter the villagers of Vrindavan from Indra's wrath is celebrated with a great offering of food — Annakut, meaning "mountain of food." In Gujarat, this day is also Bestu Varas, the new year.

## In Melbourne

The engine computes this for Melbourne's coordinates and AEDT timezone. Pratipada falls the day after the Diwali new moon, placing this celebration in mid-spring. Many Melbourne families of Gujarati and ISKCON background observe this prominently; the ISKCON temple typically holds a large Annakut display open to all.

## The story

**Short version.** Krishna persuaded the people of Vrindavan to stop their annual worship of Indra and instead honour the local land, cows, and mountain. Indra retaliated with seven days of torrential rain, and Krishna lifted Govardhan Hill on his little finger to shelter all the villagers and their animals.

**Full version.** The Bhagavata Purana (Skandha 10, Chapters 24–26) describes how the people of Vrindavan customarily performed a great sacrifice to Indra for rain. Krishna questioned this dependency and redirected the villagers' gratitude to Govardhan Hill, which sustained their cows, and to the land itself. Indra, insulted, unleashed his thunderclouds for seven days. Krishna lifted Govardhan Hill entire and held it aloft like an umbrella for seven days and nights, under which every villager, cow, and creature sheltered without harm. Indra, humbled, descended and acknowledged Krishna as supreme. To mark the victory, Krishna said the people should prepare a great mountain of cooked food — the Annakut — and offer it to the hill as gratitude. The tradition continues: ISKCON temples worldwide prepare hundreds of dishes and build them into a symbolic mound before offering them to Krishna as Govardhan.

*Sources: Bhagavata Purana (Skandha 10, Chapters 24–26)*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. Prepare any home-cooked food and place it before the shrine as an offering
2. Offer it to Krishna with a short prayer — "Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya" is sufficient
3. Take prasad with the household

### Fuller version

1. In the morning, prepare as many cooked dishes as the household can manage — the spirit is abundance, not perfection; even five or six items is fine
2. Arrange them on a tray or in a small mound at the shrine to symbolise the Govardhan hill
3. Place an image of Krishna (especially as Govardhanadhari — lifting the hill) at the shrine
4. Offer tulsi leaves, flowers, incense, and a lamp
5. Recite or sing the Govardhan Puja stuti or any Krishna bhajan; "Govardhan Dharo" kirtans are traditional
6. Perform arati
7. Distribute the prasad to family and, if possible, to neighbours
8. In some families, a small hill-shaped mound of cow-dung is formed in the yard and worshipped — representing Govardhan itself; this is especially common in rural North India
9. Gujarati families may additionally perform Chopda Pujan (blessing of account books) as part of the Bestu Varas new year ritual

### Items needed

- Home-cooked food — as many varieties as possible (rice, dal, sabzi, sweets, bread)
- Tulsi leaves
- Flowers
- Incense
- Oil lamp (diya)
- Image of Krishna

## Why

**Scriptural.** The Bhagavata Purana records Krishna's instruction to begin the Annakut tradition directly. It is one of the few places in scripture where a new practice is founded by Krishna's own command during his earthly life.

**Symbolic.** The offering of food as a mountain mirrors the hill that sheltered all living beings. The story challenges the idea that we must fear and appease powerful forces — instead, it praises self-reliance, gratitude to the land, and care for animals (the cows of Vrindavan are central to the narrative).

**Seasonal.** In India, Kartika begins after the monsoon and the first harvest. An Annakut made from fresh harvest produce was both a practical celebration and an act of gratitude. In Melbourne spring, local produce is at the start of a new growing season — a different but equally fresh timing.

## How others do it

- **Gujarat:** Bestu Varas (new year) falls on this day; families visit temples and elders, wish each other "Nutan Varshabhinandan," and begin new account books (Chopda Pujan)
- **ISKCON worldwide:** Major temple events with hundreds of dishes displayed and blessed; public darshan open to all
- **Mathura-Vrindavan:** Govardhan Hill itself is circumambulated (parikrama, approximately 21 km) by thousands of devotees
- **Maharashtra:** Called Bali Pratipada in some regions; associated with King Bali being sent to the netherworld by Vamana (an avatar of Vishnu)

## Who is traditionally exempt

There is no fast associated with Govardhan Puja — rather, it is a day of feasting and offering. Those who are unwell may offer a smaller portion and take what prasad their health allows. Children participate fully and are often given the task of arranging the food at the shrine.
