# Mattu Pongal

## In two sentences

Mattu Pongal is the third day of the Pongal festival, dedicated entirely to cattle -- the animals whose labour made the harvest possible. Cows and bulls are bathed, garlanded, painted with bright colours, fed Pongal rice, and honoured with prayers to Nandi, the sacred bull of Shiva.

## In Melbourne

Mattu Pongal falls the day after Thai Pongal, typically 15 or 16 January. Most Melbourne Tamil families do not keep cattle, so the day is adapted: some families visit farms, petting zoos, or animal sanctuaries to connect with the spirit of the day. Pet animals in the household may be garlanded and given special treats. Community Pongal events sometimes include a symbolic cattle-honouring ceremony. Collingwood Children's Farm and various petting zoos around Melbourne see Tamil families visiting around this time.

## The story

**Short version.** "Mattu" means cattle in Tamil. This day honours the animals that plough the fields and produce milk -- without them, there would be no harvest and no Pongal rice. Cattle are bathed, decorated, and fed the best food. It is a day of gratitude to the animal world.

**Full version.** The Periya Puranam tells the story of how Nandi, Shiva's bull, was sent to earth with a message for humanity: "Bathe in oil once a month, eat once a day." But Nandi, in his confusion, reversed the message: "Bathe once a day, eat three times a day." When Shiva learned of the error, he told Nandi, "Since humans now eat three times a day, you must go down to earth and help them plough enough food to sustain themselves." This is the mythological origin of cattle working the fields, and Mattu Pongal is the day humans acknowledge that debt.

The connection to Nandi runs deep in Shaiva tradition. Nandi is not merely a vehicle (vahana) but a devoted gatekeeper and first disciple of Shiva. The Nandi at the entrance of every Shiva temple faces the sanctum -- always watching, always devoted. Honouring cattle on Mattu Pongal is, by extension, honouring Nandi's devotion and service.

Jallikattu (also called Eru Thazhuvuthal, "bull-embracing") is the ancient Tamil bull-taming sport traditionally held on Mattu Pongal in villages across Tamil Nadu, especially in Madurai, Sivaganga, and Pudukkottai districts. Archaeological evidence from the Indus Valley civilisation (circa 2500 BCE) shows bull-leaping scenes, and Sangam literature describes similar events. Jallikattu is not bull-fighting -- the goal is to hold onto the bull's hump as it runs, not to harm it. It is a test of courage and a celebration of the bond between humans and cattle.

*Sources: Periya Puranam, Sangam literature, Indus Valley archaeological evidence, Tamil folk tradition*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. If you have pets, give them a special bath and a treat today
2. Offer thanks for the animals that contribute to our food -- even a moment of conscious gratitude counts
3. Feed birds or stray animals in your neighbourhood with leftover Pongal rice on a turmeric leaf
4. Light a lamp and offer a short prayer to Nandi

### Fuller version

**Morning (traditional):**
1. Rise early and bathe the cattle -- wash them thoroughly, then dry them
2. Paint their horns in bright colours (red, blue, green, yellow)
3. Garland them with marigolds and jasmine
4. Tie new bells around their necks
5. Apply kumkum (vermillion) and sandalwood paste to their foreheads
6. Feed them freshly made Pongal rice, bananas, jaggery, and sugarcane

**Melbourne adaptation:**
1. Visit a farm, petting zoo, or animal sanctuary as a family
2. If you have pets (dogs, cats, birds), bathe and groom them, tie a small garland or ribbon
3. Give pets a special meal or treat
4. Place leftover Pongal rice on a turmeric leaf outside for birds, squirrels, and other animals
5. Donate to an animal welfare organisation

**Prayer:**
1. Light a lamp before a Nandi image or at your home shrine
2. Offer turmeric, flowers, and incense
3. Recite the Pasupati mantra or Nandi prayer (see Prayers section below)

**Community:**
1. Watch Jallikattu coverage from Tamil Nadu (many channels livestream events from Alanganallur and Palamedu)
2. Share stories with children about the role of animals in agriculture and Tamil culture

### Items needed

- Flowers for garlanding (marigolds, jasmine)
- Kumkum (vermillion) and sandalwood paste
- Leftover Pongal rice, bananas, jaggery
- Turmeric leaves (for placing food offerings outside)
- Small bells or ribbons (for pets, in Melbourne)
- Lamp and incense for Nandi prayer
- Bright non-toxic paint (if decorating animal horns at a community event)

### In Melbourne

Collingwood Children's Farm, Bundoora Park Farm, and various petting zoos in the outer suburbs are popular destinations for Mattu Pongal outings. Some Tamil community groups organise events at parks where a symbolic cow or bull figure is decorated. Marigold garlands are available at Indian grocers in Dandenong and Footscray. For animal donations, Melbourne-based organisations like Edgar's Mission (farm animal sanctuary in Lancefield) or the RSPCA accept contributions.

## Why

**Scriptural.** The Periya Puranam story of Nandi's blunder establishes the sacred debt humans owe to cattle. Nandi's role as Shiva's foremost devotee elevates cattle beyond mere utility -- they are embodiments of selfless service.

**Symbolic.** Decorating cattle with garlands and paint is an inversion of the everyday relationship: for one day, the animal that serves is itself served. Feeding cattle the same Pongal rice that the family eats places the animal at the family table, symbolically. The bright colours on the horns are joy made visible.

**Seasonal.** After the harvest is gathered and the rice is cooked, it is right to turn gratitude toward the animals whose labour made it possible. Mattu Pongal completes the circle: Bhogi thanked Indra for rain, Thai Pongal thanked Surya for light, and Mattu Pongal thanks the animals for their strength.

## Prayers and Mantras

**Nandi Stuti**
ஓம் நந்தீஸ்வராய நமஃ
*Om Nandishvaraya Namah*
"Salutations to Lord Nandi."
A simple invocation to Nandi, Shiva's bull and devoted gatekeeper, recited while offering flowers and food to cattle or before a Nandi image.

**Pasupati Mantra**
ஓம் பசுபதயே நமஃ
*Om Pasupataye Namah*
"Salutations to Pasupati, the lord of all creatures."
Pasupati ("lord of animals") is a form of Shiva. This mantra honours the divine protection over all living beings -- especially appropriate on the day dedicated to cattle.

**Gratitude to Cattle (Tamil folk verse)**
மாடு இல்லா வீடு, வீடு அல்ல; மாடு வளர்த்த நாடு, நாடு ஆகும்.
*Maadu illa veedu, veedu alla; maadu valarththa naadu, naadu aagum.*
"A home without cattle is not truly a home; a land that raises cattle becomes a true nation."
A Tamil folk saying reflecting the agrarian centrality of cattle in Tamil civilisation, recited or shared on Mattu Pongal.

## How others do it

- **Andhra Pradesh / Telangana:** Called Kanuma. Cattle are decorated and worshipped. Non-vegetarian food is prepared on this day (unlike the vegetarian Sankranti day before). Cockfights were traditionally held, though now banned.
- **Karnataka:** Cattle worship is part of Sankranti celebrations. In rural areas, decorated cattle are paraded through villages. The Kambala (buffalo race) in coastal Karnataka is a parallel to Jallikattu.
- **North India:** No direct equivalent on the third day. Makar Sankranti is a single-day observance in most northern states, without the cattle-specific day.
- **Kerala:** Cattle are honoured during Vishu and Onam rather than Pongal/Sankranti.

## Who is traditionally exempt

No fasting or strenuous ritual is involved. Mattu Pongal is a day of feeding, decorating, and gratitude. Those without access to cattle simply honour the spirit through feeding birds and animals, visiting a farm, or offering a prayer to Nandi. There is no obligation that cannot be adapted.
