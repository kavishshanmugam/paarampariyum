# Navaratri Golu

## In two sentences

For nine nights in Ashvina, the Goddess is worshipped in three forms -- Durga (power), Lakshmi (sustenance), Saraswati (knowledge) -- while families display dolls (Golu/Kolu) arranged on tiered steps representing the entire cosmos. The tenth day, Vijayadashami, is the most auspicious day to begin anything new, especially a child's education (Vidyarambham).

## In Melbourne

Navaratri falls in September-October, which is mid-spring in Melbourne -- pleasant weather for visiting neighbours' Golu displays. The Tamil, Telugu, and Kannada community associations in Melbourne organise Navaratri cultural programmes, sundal evenings, and Vidyarambham ceremonies.

## The story

**Short version.** The Devi Mahatmya tells of the Goddess battling the buffalo-demon Mahishasura across nine nights, culminating in his defeat on the tenth day. In South India, rather than emphasising the battle, the focus is on the Goddess as teacher and nurturer, and on the household Golu display that depicts the cosmic order.

**Full version.** The Devi Mahatmya (Markandeya Purana, Chapters 81-93, also called Durga Saptashati) recounts Devi's battle with Mahishasura. The Devi Bhagavata Purana elaborates further.

In South India, the nine nights are divided:
- **Days 1-3:** Durga/Parvati (power, courage, removal of obstacles)
- **Days 4-6:** Lakshmi (prosperity, sustenance, generosity)
- **Days 7-9:** Saraswati (knowledge, arts, learning)

The **Golu/Kolu** is distinctly South Indian. Dolls are arranged on odd-numbered tiers (3, 5, 7, or 9 steps). The display represents the cosmos -- divine figures at the top, sages and kings in the middle, everyday village life at the bottom. Families accumulate dolls over generations; the collection grows each year. The **marapachi bommai** (unpainted wooden couple) is the essential pair in Tamil tradition, handed down through generations from a bride's mother.

The social heart of Navaratri Golu is visiting. Women visit each other's homes to see the displays, receive sundal (savoury chickpea snack) and tambulam (betel leaf packets with small gifts). This visiting tradition builds and renews community bonds.

**Saraswati Puja** on Day 9 (Mahanavami) is of supreme importance: books, musical instruments, and tools of one's trade are placed before the Goddess. No reading, writing, or use of tools on this day -- they rest in the Goddess's presence. On Vijayadashami morning, they are retrieved and work resumes.

**Vidyarambham / Aksharabhyasam** on Vijayadashami: children (age 2-5) write their first letter. An elder holds the child's hand and writes "Om" or "Hari Shree" in a plate of rice, or on the child's tongue with a gold ring. This is the most auspicious day in the year to begin education.

The **Mysore Dasara** is the most spectacular South Indian celebration -- a state festival with a torchlight parade and the Jamboo Savari (gold howdah procession on an elephant), rooted in the Vijayanagara Empire.

*Sources: Devi Mahatmya (Markandeya Purana), Devi Bhagavata Purana, Lalita Sahasranama (Brahmanda Purana), Soundarya Lahari (Adi Shankara)*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. Set up a small display of dolls or figurines on 3 tiered steps (use books or boxes covered with cloth)
2. Place divine figures at the top, everyday scenes at the bottom
3. Light a lamp before the display each evening
4. Offer sundal (boiled chickpeas with coconut and seasoning)
5. Invite someone over to see your display -- give them betel leaves, kumkum, and a small gift

### Fuller version

**Setup (Day 1 morning or day before):**
1. Build the padi (steps) -- odd-numbered: 3, 5, 7, or 9
2. Cover with cloth (white or coloured)
3. Arrange dolls: top = gods (Saraswati, Lakshmi, Parvati, Ganesha, Murugan); middle = sages, kings, epic scenes; bottom = village life, animals, modern scenes
4. Place a Kalasham (brass pot with water, mango leaves, coconut) at the centre or top
5. The marapachi bommai (wooden couple) is the essential pair if you follow Tamil tradition

**Daily routine (Days 1-9):**
1. Light the lamp morning and evening
2. Offer sundal -- a different legume each day: chickpeas, black-eyed peas, green gram, peanuts, sweet corn, rajma, etc.
3. Sing devotional songs before the display
4. Invite women to see the Golu; serve sundal; give tambulam (betel leaf + areca nut + turmeric + kumkum + bangles or small gift)
5. Visit other homes' Golu displays

**Day 7 (Saraswati Puja setup):** Place all books, instruments, and tools before the Goddess. In modern homes: laptops, tablets, car keys.

**Day 8 (Mahashtami):** Ayudha Puja (worship of tools, instruments, vehicles).

**Day 9 (Mahanavami / Saraswati Puja):** Grand Saraswati Puja. No reading, writing, or use of tools (they are with the Goddess).

**Day 10 (Vijayadashami):**
1. Retrieve books, instruments, and tools -- resume work and study
2. **Vidyarambham:** Young children write their first letter in rice, guided by an elder
3. Put away the Golu display
4. Begin new ventures -- Vijayadashami is the most auspicious day to start anything new

### Items needed

- Steps/shelving for the padi (books, boxes, or dedicated wooden steps)
- Cloth to cover steps
- Dolls/figurines -- accumulated over years; starter sets available at Indian stores
- Marapachi bommai (traditional wooden couple) if following Tamil tradition
- Kalasham (brass pot), mango leaves, coconut
- Sundal ingredients: multiple legumes, grated coconut, mustard seeds, curry leaves, dried red chili
- Betel leaves, areca nut, kumkum, turmeric, small gifts for tambulam
- Lamp, incense, flowers daily

### In Melbourne

Dolls can be sourced from Indian stores in Dandenong and Footscray before Navaratri, or ordered online from India. Many Melbourne Tamil/Telugu/Kannada families bring dolls from India over the years. Betel leaves are available at most Indian/Sri Lankan grocers year-round. The Tamil and Telugu community associations run Navaratri programmes with collective Golu displays and cultural events.

## Why

**Scriptural.** The Devi Mahatmya prescribes worship of the Goddess across nine nights. The Lalita Sahasranama enumerates her thousand names. The Soundarya Lahari (attributed to Adi Shankara) is recited during Navaratri.

**Symbolic.** The three-fold progression (Durga, Lakshmi, Saraswati) maps stages of growth: first courage and removal of obstacles, then sustenance and resources, then knowledge and wisdom. The Golu is a microcosm -- the entire universe arranged under one roof, all illuminated by the same lamp.

**Seasonal.** In India, Sharad Navaratri falls after the monsoon when the weather clears. In Melbourne, October is mid-spring.

## Prayers and Mantras

**Saraswati Vandana**
ஸரஸ்வதி நமஸ்துப்யம் வரதே காமரூபிணி |
வித்யாரம்பம் கரிஷ்யாமி ஸித்திர் பவது மே ஸதா ||
*Saraswati Namastubhyam Varade Kamarupini | Vidyarambham Karishyami Siddhir Bhavatu Me Sada*
"Salutations to Saraswati, giver of boons. I begin my studies; may success always be mine."
Recited on Vijayadashami during the Vidyarambham (initiation of learning) ceremony for children.

**Mahishasura Mardini Stotram** (refrain)
ஐயிகிரி நந்தினி நந்திதமேதினி
*Ayigiri Nandini Nanditamedini*
"O daughter of the mountain, you who bring joy to the earth..."
The famous Mahishasura Mardini Stotram is sung during Navaratri evenings, celebrating Durga's victory.

## How others do it

- **Andhra Pradesh / Telangana:** Bommala Koluvu (doll festival); Batukamma flower festival runs concurrently in Telangana -- women arrange flower stacks and float them in water
- **Karnataka:** Mysore Dasara; Bombe Habba at homes; Shami Puja on Vijayadashami (exchange of shami/banni leaves as "gold")
- **Kerala:** Emphasis on Saraswati Puja and Vidyarambham; books placed before Saraswati on Day 8 (Pooja Veypu), retrieved on Day 10
- **North India:** Ramlila performances; Dussehra effigy burning (Ravana); Durga Puja pandals in Bengal -- very different from the South Indian observance

## Who is traditionally exempt

In South India, many do not fast during Navaratri (unlike North India where 9-day fasting is common). When fasting is observed, children, the elderly, pregnant/nursing women, and the ill are exempt. The Devi Bhagavata states that the Goddess is pleased by devotion, not by deprivation.
