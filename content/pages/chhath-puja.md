# Chhath Puja

## In two sentences

A four-day festival of sun worship, observed mainly in Bihar, Jharkhand, and eastern UP, culminating on the Shashthi of Shukla Paksha in Kartika with offerings to the setting and rising sun while standing in water. It is one of the few Vedic festivals that has survived essentially unchanged, requiring no priest and no idol.

## In Melbourne

The engine computes this for Melbourne's coordinates and AEDT timezone. Chhath falls about six days after Diwali, in mid to late November in Melbourne, when the days are long and warm. The tradition requires a natural water body -- families in Melbourne sometimes use the beach at St Kilda or Brighton, or gather at a lake. Sunrise is around 5:55 AEDT.

## The story

**Short version.** Chhath is dedicated to Surya (the Sun) and Chhathi Maiya (Usha, the wife of Surya). The devotee thanks the sun for sustaining life on earth and prays for wellbeing and prosperity.

**Full version.** The Rigveda contains hymns to Surya and Usha (Dawn), and Chhath is understood as a continuation of this ancient sun worship. The Mahabharata describes Draupadi performing Chhath on the advice of the sage Dhaumya. Karna, the son of Surya, is also associated with this observance. The festival's distinctiveness lies in its directness -- no temples, no priests, no murtis. The devotee (called a vrati) offers arghya (water libation) directly to the sun at the riverbank, standing waist-deep in water, at both sunset and sunrise. This is one of the most physically demanding Hindu observances, involving a 36-hour waterless fast. The offering of thekua (a wheat-jaggery sweet) and fruit arranged on bamboo baskets (soop) is iconic.

*Sources: Rigveda (Surya Sukta), Mahabharata (Vana Parva), regional folk tradition*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. On the evening of the Shashthi, face the setting sun near any body of water
2. Offer water with both hands towards the sun (arghya)
3. Place fruit and a lit diya on a plate, hold it up towards the sun
4. Say a short prayer to Surya: "Om Suryaya Namah"
5. The next morning at dawn, repeat facing the rising sun

### Fuller version

1. **Day 1 (Nahay Khay, Chaturthi):** Bathe in a river or lake. Cook and eat only one meal of rice, dal, and gourd (lauki)
2. **Day 2 (Kharna, Panchami):** Fast all day. In the evening, break fast with kheer (rice pudding) and chapati. This is the last meal before the 36-hour fast
3. **Day 3 (Sandhya Arghya, Shashthi evening):** Prepare the soop (bamboo tray) with thekua, fruit, and sugarcane. Go to the riverbank at sunset, stand in the water, and offer arghya to the setting sun
4. **Day 4 (Usha Arghya, Saptami morning):** Before dawn, return to the water. Offer arghya to the rising sun. Break the fast after this offering

### Items needed

- Bamboo tray (soop) or a large plate
- Thekua (wheat-jaggery-ghee sweet, baked or fried)
- Seasonal fruit (banana, coconut, sugarcane, sweet lime)
- Small oil lamps (diyas)
- New cloth to wear during the offering
- A natural water body (river, lake, or sea)

### In Melbourne

Fresh sugarcane can sometimes be found at Asian grocers in Footscray or Springvale. Thekua ingredients are standard (wheat flour, jaggery, ghee). Brighton Beach and St Kilda Beach have been used by Melbourne's Bihari community for Chhath gatherings.

## Why

**Scriptural.** The Rigveda's Surya Sukta praises the sun as the source of all life. Chhath continues this direct, priestless worship.

**Symbolic.** Standing in water and offering to the setting sun (which will return) expresses gratitude and faith. The rising sun arghya completes the cycle.

**Seasonal.** In India, Chhath marks the transition into early winter after the harvest. The offerings of grain and fruit are the season's first yield.

## How others do it

- **Bihar / Jharkhand:** The heartland of Chhath. Entire communities gather at ghats. The atmosphere is deeply communal
- **Delhi / Mumbai:** Large Chhath gatherings at Yamuna and Juhu, organised by the Bihari diaspora
- **Nepal (Terai):** Chhath is a public holiday, observed much as in Bihar
- **Diaspora:** In the US, UK, and Australia, communities gather at lakes and beaches

## Who is traditionally exempt

The 36-hour fast is extremely strict. Pregnant women, nursing mothers, the elderly, children, and anyone unwell should not attempt it. Watching and helping the vrati is itself considered meritorious.
