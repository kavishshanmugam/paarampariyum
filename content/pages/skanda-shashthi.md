# Skanda Shashthi

## In two sentences

A six-day fast and prayer to Murugan, culminating on the Shashthi of Shukla Paksha in Aippasi (October-November) with the Soorasamharam -- the dramatic re-enactment of Murugan's victory over Soorapadman. The Kanda Sashti Kavasam, a protective hymn recited throughout, is one of the most widely known Tamil devotional texts.

## In Melbourne

Skanda Shashthi falls in October-November, spring in Melbourne. Tamil temples and community groups may organise a Soorasamharam re-enactment. The Sri Lankan Tamil community is particularly active in observing this festival.

## The story

**Short version.** The same Soorapadman battle as Thaipusam, but this festival marks the specific six-day war. On the sixth day, Murugan's Vel splits Soorapadman -- who had hidden as a mango tree -- into a peacock and a rooster. The dramatic re-enactment at temples is the climactic event.

**Full version.** The Kanda Puranam (Tamil) details the six-day battle in sequential episodes. Each day's battle is recounted at temples during the festival. On the final day, Soorapadman takes the form of a massive mango tree to evade Murugan. Murugan hurls the Vel, splitting the tree. The halves become his peacock (vahana) and rooster (flag). The Soorasamharam re-enactment at temples like Tiruchendur features elaborate stage combat.

The **Kanda Sashti Kavasam** (by Devaraya Swamigal, 19th century) is the defining hymn of this period. It describes Murugan's Vel protecting every part of the body from head to toe. Not a Vedic text but a Tamil devotional composition that has become ubiquitous -- most Tamil Hindus know at least the opening lines.

On Day 7 (Saptami), the festival concludes with Thiru Kalyanam -- Murugan's celestial wedding to Devasena (Devayani), daughter of Indra.

*Sources: Skanda Purana, Kanda Puranam (Kachiyappa Sivachariyar), Kanda Sashti Kavasam (Devaraya Swamigal)*

## What to do

### Ten-minute version

1. Recite or listen to the Kanda Sashti Kavasam (widely available as audio online)
2. Light a lamp and offer flowers to Murugan
3. Eat a simple vegetarian meal (no onion/garlic during the 6-day period)
4. Visit a Murugan temple if possible

### Fuller version

**Days 1-5:** Strict fast -- one meal a day (some take only fruit or liquid); no onion, garlic, meat, alcohol. Daily recitation of Kanda Sashti Kavasam (ideally 6 times per day). Daily temple visit if possible.

**Day 6 (Shashthi -- Soorasamharam):** Wake early; bathe; visit the temple for the Soorasamharam re-enactment at sunset. The Vel is thrust into a representation of the mango tree. This is the most dramatic moment of the festival calendar.

**Day 7 (Saptami -- Thiru Kalyanam):** Murugan's wedding to Devasena. The fast is broken. A grand feast follows.

### Items needed

- Image or murti of Murugan with Vel
- Flowers (red and yellow), fruits
- Camphor, incense
- Kanda Sashti Kavasam text or audio
- Simple vegetarian food for the fast days

### In Melbourne

Audio and video of Kanda Sashti Kavasam is freely available on YouTube and Spotify. Tamil community groups and the Sri Lankan Tamil community may organise Soorasamharam events. Sri Siva Vishnu Temple (Carrum Downs) holds Shaiva festival programmes.

## Why

**Scriptural.** One of the most important Shaiva observances in Tamil tradition. The Tirumurugaatruppadai (Sangam literature) and the Skanda Purana authorise Murugan worship.

**Symbolic.** Soorapadman = the ego that takes many forms to evade destruction. The mango tree form = ego disguised as natural/normal. The Vel = discriminative wisdom (viveka) that pierces all disguises.

## Prayers and Mantras

### Shadakshara Mantra (Six-Syllable Mantra)
**Tamil:** ஓம் சரவணபவ
**Transliteration:** Om Saravana Bhava
**Meaning:** "Om, he who was born in the Saravana lake (among the six-star clusters)."
Murugan's most sacred mantra, encoding his origin from the six sparks of Shiva's third eye that fell into the Saravana lake and became six infants, later united as Shanmukha (six-faced). Chanted 108 times during Skanda Shashthi and daily Murugan worship. In Tamil Shaiva tradition, "Saravana Bhava" is considered the supreme mantra -- Murugan himself whispered its meaning into Shiva's ear at Swamimalai.

### Shanmukha Gayatri
**Tamil:** ஓம் தத்புருஷாய வித்மஹே மஹாசேனாய தீமஹி | தன்னோ ஷண்முக ப்ரசோதயாத் ||
**Transliteration:** Om Tatpurushaya Vidmahe Mahasenaya Dheemahi | Tanno Shanmukha Prachodayat
**Meaning:** "Om, we meditate on the supreme being, the great commander of the divine army; may that six-faced one inspire and illumine us."
The Gayatri-metre invocation of Murugan, used in formal temple and home puja. "Mahasena" (great commander) refers to Murugan's role as the general of the Devas in the war against Soorapadman.

### Kandha Sashti Kavasam (Opening Verse)
**Tamil:** அமரர் இடர் தீர அமரம் புரிந்த குமரனடி நெஞ்சே குறி
**Transliteration:** Amarar idar theera amaram purintha kumaranaip potruthuthal naal kadan
**Meaning:** "It is our daily duty to praise the feet of Kumara (Murugan), who waged war to end the suffering of the Devas."
The invocatory verse of the Kanda Sashti Kavasam by Devaraya Swamigal (19th century). The full Kavasam is a protective armour-hymn describing Murugan's Vel guarding every part of the devotee's body. Recited six times daily during the six days of Skanda Shashthi, and widely recited as a daily prayer in Tamil households.

## How others do it

- **Tamil Nadu:** Major temples (Tiruchendur, Palani, Tiruttani) have elaborate 6-day festivals with Soorasamharam
- **Sri Lanka:** 25-day festival at Nallur Kandaswamy Temple, Jaffna
- **Not observed in North India**

## Who is traditionally exempt

The 6-day fast is strenuous. Children under 12, the elderly, pregnant/nursing women, the ill, and those on medication should not fast (or should take a modified fast -- fruit and milk). The fast is voluntary; temple visits and Kavasam recitation are available to all.
